# Studies on the genus *Ornithoptera*: preliminary remarks on the phylogenetic relationships among *O. alexandrae*, *O. victoriae* and *O. priamus* [Papilionidae]

**by Patrick Blandin**
*Alexanor*, VIII, 1973 (pp. 63–?)

The Ornithoptera have given rise to various phylogenetic considerations. The principal results were obtained by **Zeuner** (3), whose conclusions were subsequently modified by **Schmid** (2); the latter author attached great importance to *O. allotei*, which led him to conceive differently the relationships between the *priamus* group (*priamus*, *croesus*, *aesacus*) and the *victoriae* group (*victoriae*, *allotei*, *alexandrae*).

For lepidopterists familiar with the Ornithoptera, the membership of *alexandrae* in the *victoriae* group is a self-evident fact, resting on the remarkable resemblance in the shape of the males. However, no detailed [study] of this has ever been carried out, and Zeuner himself minimised the importance of certain characters common to *priamus* and *alexandrae*. Yet **Jordan** (1) had pointed some of them out — in particular the fact that the arrangement of the R3, R4 and R5 veins of the forewings is the same in *priamus* and *alexandrae*, whereas it is different in *victoriae*. This fact alone was not enough to call the classical conceptions into question; it nevertheless prompted me to take up the problem again by undertaking a rigorous comparative study of the three species.

In order to compare, point by point, the wing venation and its modifications as a function of wing shape, I was led to define ratios expressing the proportions of the cells and the relative lengths of certain veins. Measurements were taken on every specimen I was able to obtain, so as to yield statistically valid biometric results. The characters of the wing pattern (decoration) were studied by comparison with the characters common to the various Ornithoptera species that are regarded as the most fundamental. All of this was done in the females as much as in the males, the females having [been neglected] by previous authors. The ornamentation of the body was likewise studied, as were the male genitalia.

The detail of these investigations will be the subject of a later publication; its technical nature has led me to present beforehand the essential results and the conclusions to which they lead.

The observations made show that the resemblance between the males of *alexandrae* and *victoriae* is superficial. By the shape of the forewing cell, and by the fact that R3 arises from it independently of R4 and R5, *alexandrae* appears very close to *priamus*: the differences between these two species have nothing to do with those that *victoriae* shows with respect to *priamus*. This is very clear for the elongation of the hindwing cell, which is achieved in a completely different way in *alexandrae* and in *victoriae*. Although less pronounced, essentially the same facts are observed in the females.

Relative to *priamus*, the wing pattern of the males of *alexandrae* and *victoriae* is obviously much modified, but in different directions. *O. alexandrae* retains the three coloured bands of the forewings known in *priamus* and in the species of the *chimaerae* group; in *victoriae* the arrangement of the coloured areas is quite different. The position of the androconial area is the same in *priamus* and *alexandrae*, [but] different in *victoriae*. *O. alexandrae* and *priamus* possess a red thoracic pile, absent in *victoriae*, whose abdomen on the other hand bears a black median ventral band. The only characters common to the males of *alexandrae* and *victoriae* (and sometimes also to *priamus*) are the absence of black discal spots on the hindwings and the absence of a metallic mesonotal band (which is sometimes much reduced in certain forms of *priamus*).

Observations of the same kind can be made in the females, *victoriae* being further distinguished from the two other species by several characters (yellow colouring in the basal parts of the wings, presence of scales with metallic reflections, etc.).

The male genitalia are very different among the three species, *alexandrae* having retained the most primitive characters (3).

The abundance and importance of the characters common to *priamus* and *alexandrae* — generally opposable to those of *victoriae* — suggest that these two species proceed from a single lineage, separated at an early stage from the one that gave rise to *victoriae*. A strong divergence then became established between *priamus* and *alexandrae*, the latter species having either retained primitive characters (genitalia) or acquired characters of its own, often opposable to those that appeared in the *victoriae* lineage. At most one might allow that these two separate lines of evolution resulted, through pure convergence, in an overall resemblance in the form of the males.

**The *victoriae* group must therefore be reduced to *victoriae* and *allotei*** (if the latter is not a hybrid *priamus urvilleanus* × *victoriae regis*), **while *alexandrae* should be placed in the vicinity of the *priamus* group**, of which it constitutes a very strongly marked differentiation.
